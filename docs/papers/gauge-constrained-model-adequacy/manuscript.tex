\begin{abstract}
Model-adequacy decisions for finite operator families depend on the declared class of
admissible alignments. This working paper organizes machine-checked finite results that
distinguish identity-only, global-unitary, and complete pointwise-unitary alignment
families; prove forward feasibility transfer and exact failures of the reverse
implication; separate fixed-unitary Fourier equivariance from pointwise-gauge changes of
Fourier coefficients; and connect admissible-set geometry to exact and interval-valued
finite decisions. The individual ingredients are largely standard finite algebra and
set logic. Their audit-first composition gives a family-relative adequacy framework with
explicit checker boundaries. An exact two-point rank-two witness proves that a
pointwise gauge transformation need not commute with zero-mode Fourier truncation, and
finite tail theorems identify the deliberate-truncation root-mean-square error and bound
its pointwise and maximum ambient norms.
\end{abstract}
