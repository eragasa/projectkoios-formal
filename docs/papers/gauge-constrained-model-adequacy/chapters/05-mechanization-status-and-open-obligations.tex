\section{The compatibility problem recovered}

The central conclusion of this companion is that spectral and operator reduction must be
posed as a common-model problem.  For each declared candidate class
$\mathfrak M_j$, spectral and aligned-operator criteria define two admissible sets.  The
primary exact question is whether those sets contain one common Hamiltonian.  When they
do not, an exact or certified lower bound on their metric separation can quantify the
obstruction under the declared geometry.  Repeating the decision over a finite ordered
hierarchy identifies the first tested class that passes the joint criterion.

This formulation keeps the quantifiers visible.  One spectrally successful model and a
different operator-successful model do not prove compatibility.  One favorable
alignment for a fixed candidate is only a witness inside the operator branch.  A
locality diagnostic, a support choice, and a certificate must also refer to the same
candidate whenever the decision contract requires a common endpoint.

The alignment and Fourier results should therefore be read in their proper role.  They
define and constrain $\mathfrak A_{H,j}$; they do not replace
$\mathfrak A_{E,j}\cap\mathfrak A_{H,j}\neq\varnothing$ as the research question.

\section{What the exact finite theory contributes}

The finite-frame sequence distinguishes retained subspaces from retained coordinates.
Projectors are invariant under right-unitary frame changes, compressed matrices are
covariant, and reconstructed ambient operators are invariant.  These facts establish
which differences are physical candidates for an operator residual and which are merely
coordinate changes.

The aligned-difference and family theorems make the operator criterion
family-relative.  Identity, global-unitary, and complete pointwise-unitary families
answer different questions.  Inclusion gives one-way feasibility transfer; strictness
witnesses disprove reverse transfer in general.

The Fourier theorems identify a second structural boundary.  One common unitary
commutes with finite Fourier coefficient extraction and exact support truncation.
Pointwise conjugation preserves pointwise unitary-invariant power but can redistribute
Fourier weight.  The three-channel calculus places deliberate truncation between an
interpolant and its support-restricted approximation, proves the exact vector
decomposition of total residual, and bounds its pointwise, maximum, and root-mean-square
norms.  The exact rank-two counterexample demonstrates gauge--truncation
noncommutation, while the finite-tail results give positive control only of the
deliberate-truncation leg after a support has been declared.

The geometry and decision theorems address the outer compatibility layer.  Closed-ball,
rank-two ellipse, and rectangle families provide exact intersection and separation
criteria under explicit hypotheses.  The generic finite compatibility schema now
packages model and candidate inventories, central infimum-based compatibility, stronger
common-alignment witness compatibility, explicit conformant model embeddings, metric
separation, and first-compatible selection.  Certificate soundness translates accepted
finite data into narrower propositions.  Three-way intervals preserve threshold overlap
as unresolved, and first-passage logic selects an earliest passing element of a complete
finite inventory without assuming monotonicity.

Together these results form a substantial reusable basis for the ICMSEP problem.  They
still do not amount to an end-to-end theorem for the proposed silicon study because no
application-specific losses, inventories, tolerances, embeddings, channel predicates, or
retained certificate are supplied.

\section{Development of the error-channel program}
\label{sec:seven-paper-error-channel-development}

The seven-paper research program uses the three-channel calculus as a recurring
approximation core, not as a universal partition of every uncertainty.  In the first
controlled rank-two study, the decomposition is operational: reference-to-interpolant,
interpolant-to-truncated, and reference-to-truncated consequences are reported
separately.  Cyclic conversion is retained as an additional diagnostic rather than
being folded into one of those three channels.  The second study carries the same core
across spatial dimension, lattice geometry, complete physical-support inventories, and
synthetic strain.  There the separate channels help distinguish whether a final error
floor comes from finite interpolation or from the deliberate support restriction.

The multiband program makes the limits of the three-channel description explicit.
Projection, disentanglement, downfolding, frame selection, alignment-family restriction,
finite conversion, interpolation, and truncation are different operations.  The final
interpolation--truncation--total chain remains available once three conformant fields
have been constructed, but it is nested inside this finer ledger.  A successful
three-channel identity does not certify the upstream projector, gauge, or alignment.

The first-principles silicon study changes scale again.  Its top-level proposed budget
separates parent-model discrepancy, numerical or discretization error, local-extraction
error, and model-reduction error.  The three-channel calculus can support the localized
representation and support-truncation subproblem, but it cannot replace those four
application-level categories.  Moreover, the study is pre-results: no production
silicon evidence or proposition-specific Formal certificate is supplied here.

For pristine--defect subtraction, an aligned difference is meaningful only after the
two operators have been placed on a qualified common retained space with a common
scalar energy reference.  Finite-size, projection, localization, alignment,
energy-reference, transform, and truncation errors must remain visible.  The obstruction
program adds a still earlier gate: if a required global or symmetry-compatible
alignment cannot be constructed, the intended global aligned residual may be
undefined, so a numerical three-channel report cannot repair the missing comparison
map.  The final spin--orbit impurity program may use nested three-channel decompositions
inside qualified model classes only after matched pristine and doped SOC branches,
common-space alignment, and energy referencing have passed their own gates.

This development gives the residual calculus a precise programmatic role.  It is a
transversal verification spine for conformant approximation chains, subordinate to the
common-Hamiltonian compatibility question and embedded within broader error ontologies
where the science requires them.  The immutable owner-source identities, paper-by-paper
roles, and current evidence boundaries are recorded in
Appendix~\ref{app:seven-paper-error-channel-crosswalk}.  Papers D--G remain pre-results
or prospective at that source snapshot; no numerical or publication status transfers
from this synthesis.

\section{Current mechanization boundary}

The current Formal snapshot uses Lean~4~\cite{DeMouraUllrich2021} and the Mathlib
library~\cite{MathlibCommunity2020} to prove the component theorem families listed in
Appendix~\ref{app:formal-crosswalk} and packages their outer finite logic in
\decl{FiniteCompatibilitySchema}. Exact toolchain and dependency revisions, rather than
bibliographic references, control reproducibility. The schema contains finite model and candidate
inventories, spectral and aligned-operator losses and budgets, alignment families,
mandatory channel predicates, central and witness compatibility, conformant embeddings,
metric separation, and first-compatible selection.

The snapshot does not instantiate that schema with the scientific data required by the
proposed study.  It still lacks:

\begin{itemize}
  \item a frozen application-specific model inventory and its external-coverage claim;
  \item candidate maps tying exact schema values to the intended Hamiltonians;
  \item spectral observables, aligned operator losses, weights, supports, and tolerances
        selected and justified for the application;
  \item proposition-specific channel predicates binding one common support, alignment,
        intermediate field, sign convention, and evaluation inventory;
  \item checked conformant embeddings between the concrete model classes;
  \item compactness, closedness, or direct finite geometry needed for any converse from
        zero separation to intersection;
  \item a certificate covering the complete declared admissible sets; and
  \item retained exact or outward-rounded data satisfying that certificate contract.
\end{itemize}

Nor does the snapshot contain a retained runtime certificate for a bulk-silicon DFT or
Wannier calculation.  The current checked-certificate count at that external boundary
is zero.  The proposed first-principles parent, numerical fitting, withheld validation,
and material interpretation remain outside the formal result.

This boundary is not a defect in the generic schema.  It records the remaining work
needed to instantiate exact finite logic as a proposition-specific adequacy decision.

\section{Priority open finite problems}

The following extensions are ordered by their relationship to the central compatibility
question.

\begin{openproblem}[Proposition-specific schema instantiation]
Instantiate the generic finite compatibility schema with one frozen model inventory,
exact candidate representation, spectral and aligned-operator losses, finite budgets,
alignment families, and mandatory conformant channel predicates.  Bind the application
objects to the intended Hamiltonians without importing runtime authority into Lean.
\end{openproblem}

The instantiation must preserve the distinction between the central infimum criterion
and the stronger common-alignment witness predicate.  It must not assume that runtime
optimization found a global minimum or that nominal model nesting supplies a conformant
embedding.

\begin{openproblem}[Heterogeneous model carriers]
The first generic schema uses one shared candidate carrier and one shared alignment
carrier across model classes.  Develop a dependent or explicitly transported extension
only if a concrete consumer cannot use a reviewed common carrier.  Any such extension
must retain the current common-candidate, attainment, channel-conformance, and embedding
boundaries.
\end{openproblem}

\begin{openproblem}[General attained separation]
Package the compactness, closedness, nonemptiness, and metric hypotheses under which zero
set distance is equivalent to intersection and positive distance is attained.  Connect
that result to the finite compatibility schema without identifying a positive map
tolerance with literal intersection.
\end{openproblem}

\begin{openproblem}[Proposition-specific compatibility certificate]
Specify exact certificate data that can establish either one common admissible candidate
or a positive lower bound separating the complete declared admissible sets.  The checker
contract must bind the model class, loss definitions, tolerances, metric, alignment
family, and coverage claim.
\end{openproblem}

\begin{openproblem}[Operator-norm equivariance]
Establish invariance of the induced Euclidean operator norm under finite unitary
conjugation and package it for aligned operator differences.  This would support
operator-norm criteria without changing the alignment-family semantics.
\end{openproblem}

\begin{openproblem}[Conformant two-path residuals]
Formulate a two-path comparison in which both routes use the same admissible alignment,
and prove gauge invariance of the resulting residual under explicit conformity
hypotheses.  Separately selected gauges define different routes.
\end{openproblem}

\begin{openproblem}[Pointwise-gauge convolution]
Derive a general finite convolution formula for Fourier coefficients under pointwise
unitary conjugation, with support consequences specializing to the proved two-point
counterexample.  The theorem should remain finite and should not be presented as an
infinite-volume decay result.
\end{openproblem}

\begin{openproblem}[Shell-resolved fixed-unitary invariance]
For a declared real-space shell decomposition, prove Frobenius invariance under one
constant unitary acting internally on retained coordinates without mixing shell labels.
\end{openproblem}

\begin{openproblem}[Finite elimination]
Develop exact finite Feshbach or Schur-complement elimination separately from
spectral-gap and norm-bound certificates.  State invertibility and domain hypotheses
explicitly and do not infer infinite-volume consequences from the finite formula.
\end{openproblem}

\begin{openproblem}[Projection-induced support growth]
Prove a bounded finite support-growth statement for a specified projection mechanism.
Quantify the finite support relation without claiming exponential decay, continuum
localization, or topology.
\end{openproblem}

\section{Analytical, numerical, and scientific questions beyond the finite theory}

Several required questions lie outside the present exact finite theorem basis:

\begin{itemize}
  \item construction and convergence of the first-principles parent Hamiltonian;
  \item validation of the selected Wannier subspace and its real-space decay;
  \item scientific justification of spectral observables, weights, and tolerances;
  \item completeness of the tested model hierarchy;
  \item global correctness of nonlinear fitting and alignment optimization;
  \item exact certification of runtime orthonormality and interval containment;
  \item withheld-wavevector and material-specific validation;
  \item asymptotic locality, continuum crossover, and infinite-volume convergence;
  \item Chern integrality, branch unwrapping, and Wannier obstruction theorems; and
  \item novelty, publication readiness, and independent scientific acceptance.
\end{itemize}

These questions are independent.  Closing one does not close the others.  A checked
finite Fourier identity does not validate a DFT calculation; a converged numerical
calculation does not prove that its binary64 output satisfies an exact certificate; and
a common finite candidate does not establish that the candidate class is physically
complete.

\section{Role of the proof companion}

This document is a formal proof companion to the spectral--operator model-adequacy
program.  Its purpose is to expose the exact mathematical structure, show which
component results are already machine checked, and identify the missing bridges without
promoting prospective application claims into theorems.

A shorter scientific publication may select only the results needed for one narrative.
Such extraction should begin with the common-model compatibility question, not with the
alignment machinery.  Alignment families, Fourier locality, and certificate logic then
appear where they support the operator criterion, separation result, and model-selection
decision.

The appendices preserve declaration identities, immutable source provenance, and
notation bridges for that later extraction.  Editorial compression may shorten the
argument; it may not change the quantifier structure, replace separate witnesses by a
common one, or strengthen the finite theorem basis into a material-validation claim.
