\chapter{Research Question, Proof Architecture, and Authority}
\label{ch:proof-architecture}

\section{Research question}

Let $\mathcal U$ be a declared family of unitary alignment fields and let
$\operatorname{Accept}(U)$ be an exact adequacy predicate. The central question is not
whether some unrestricted coordinate transformation can improve a comparison. It is
whether
\[
  \exists U\in\mathcal U,\;\operatorname{Accept}(U)
\]
holds for the declared family, and which Fourier-locality and certificate conclusions
remain valid under that same family.

The paper connects three finite facts. First, feasibility is monotone under
proved inclusion of alignment families, while strict inclusion admits exact failures of
the reverse implication. Second, one fixed unitary commutes with the finite Fourier and
support-truncation operations used here, whereas a varying pointwise unitary can change
Fourier coefficients. Third, exact and interval-valued checkers can classify a complete
supplied finite inventory without treating threshold overlap or an incomplete search as
a decisive outcome.

The individual ingredients are mostly standard. Their formal, family-relative
composition is the organizing contribution of this paper.

\section{Purpose and proof boundary}

The proof program starts from exact propositions. Each theorem has the scope expressed
by its Lean type and explicit assumptions. Runtime conformance, physical applicability,
and publication assessment are separate evidence questions.

This paper explains the dependency structure, assumptions, and mathematical role of the
Formal theorem families. Declaration status remains in the theorem manifest.

\section{Authority order}

Four Formal-owned surfaces have distinct roles.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Lean source under \modulepath{ProjectKoios/} owns exact definitions,
        propositions, and proof terms.
  \item \modulepath{docs/theorem-manifest.md} owns stable declaration identifiers and
        repository status.
  \item Files under \modulepath{proofs/} explain individual statements, provenance,
        assumptions, and limitations.
  \item This manuscript synthesizes theorem families into a readable proof program.
\end{enumerate}

External manuscripts, calculation plans, schemas, and research notes provide
motivation. Formal source fixes the theorem statements and status. Later exports name an
immutable Formal revision and preserve the declaration identifiers used.

\section{Independent status axes}

The proof program keeps the following questions separate:

\begin{itemize}
  \item Is an exact proposition implemented and proved in Lean?
  \item Which axioms does the compiled declaration report?
  \item Has a human-readable proof dossier been written and reviewed?
  \item Has an external certificate schema been frozen and independently verified?
  \item Does retained numerical evidence satisfy that schema?
  \item Are the assumptions applicable to the intended scientific system?
  \item Has novelty, publication, or human acceptance been established?
\end{itemize}

Each axis requires its own evidence. A clean Lean build establishes the compiled
propositions; runtime arrays, continuum limits, and material models require their own
verification and validation records.

\section{Core dependency structure}

The present exact program is usefully read in six layers.

\begin{description}
  \item[Finite representation.] Matrices, finite frames, Hermitian operators,
    compression, folding, and coordinate changes.
  \item[Alignment and covariance.] Sewing data, aligned pullback, aligned differences,
    and admissible alignment families.
  \item[Fourier and locality.] Finite Abelian transforms, reconstruction, Parseval
    identities, truncation, and fixed-versus-pointwise gauge behavior.
  \item[Loss geometry.] Spectral and operator residuals, admissible sets, Hilbert balls,
    rank-two ellipses, and exact separation.
  \item[Certificates and decisions.] Exact Boolean checkers, interval dispositions,
    Pareto predicates, and finite first-passing semantics.
  \item[Special finite models.] Rank-one resolvent defects, rank-two perturbation,
    tight-binding constructions, gauge loops, and projector--Bargmann identities.
\end{description}

The layers form a dependency graph. Each application selects the relevant path and
supplies the objects and assumptions required by its theorems.

\section{Difference convention}

This companion uses the existing Formal convention
\[
  \Delta_{\mathrm{ref},\mathrm{cand}}(k)
  =H_{\mathrm{ref}}(k)-U(k)^{-1}H_{\mathrm{cand}}(k)U(k).
\]
Reversing the operand order negates the difference. Any downstream narrative using the
opposite defect convention must record the sign bridge explicitly. A change of sign is
algebraically simple but can change the interpretation of an impurity potential or
perturbation.

\section{Export rule}

An exported chapter is a narrative snapshot derived from a pinned Formal revision. Its
record contains the commit, source tree, declaration IDs, assumptions, known axioms, and
open external obligations. Scientific interpretation receives a separate review.
