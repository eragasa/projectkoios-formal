\section{Exact mathematical statement}
Let $i:\Phi\to\mathcal H$ be the injective dense inclusion of a
\texttt{RiggedHilbertSpaceCore}. Let $A,B:\Phi\to\Phi$ be complex linear maps satisfying
\[
  \langle i(A\phi),i\psi\rangle
  =\langle i\phi,i(B\psi)\rangle
  \qquad(\phi,\psi\in\Phi).
\]
For the induced densely defined partial operators $T_A$ and $T_B$ on $\mathcal H$, one has
\[
  T_B\leq T_A^{\dagger},
\]
where $T_A^{\dagger}$ is Mathlib's maximal adjoint partial operator and $\leq$ is partial-operator
extension order.

\section{Source, assumptions, and statement delta}
This is a source-neutral consequence of Mathlib's adjoint API. The test-space inner-product
identity is supplied as a hypothesis. Dense domain follows from the declared dense inclusion. The
statement is containment only: it does not say $T_B=T_A^{\dagger}$, $T_A=T_A^{\dagger}$, or that
either operator is closed, closable, symmetric, essentially self-adjoint, or self-adjoint.

\section{Prior art and contribution boundary}
A formally adjoint operator on a dense domain is contained in the corresponding maximal adjoint;
this is standard operator theory and is implemented by Mathlib. The repository contribution is the
rigged-test-space specialization and explicit scope boundary, not a novelty claim.

\section{Proof schematic}
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\mathcal D(T_A)\text{ dense},\\
&\langle T_Ax,y\rangle=\langle x,T_By\rangle
  \quad(x\in\mathcal D(T_A),\ y\in\mathcal D(T_B))
\\[2pt]
&\hspace{38mm}\Longrightarrow\quad T_B\leq T_A^{\dagger}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The first premise is \texttt{QM-OPERATOR-THM-001}; the second is
\texttt{QM-OPERATOR-THM-002}.

\section{Human-readable proof}
The supplied test-space identity lifts to a formal-adjoint relation between $T_A$ and $T_B$.
The domain of $T_A$ is dense because it is the range of the dense test-space inclusion. By the
maximal property of the adjoint partial operator, every $y$ in the domain of $T_B$ satisfies the
adjoint-domain condition for $T_A$, and $T_A^{\dagger}y=T_By$. Therefore $T_B$ is a restriction of
$T_A^{\dagger}$.
