\section{Exact mathematical statement}
Let $\Phi$ be the declared locally convex test space, let $\mathcal H$ be a complex Hilbert space,
and let $i:\Phi\to\mathcal H$ be the continuous injective inclusion supplied by a
\texttt{RiggedHilbertSpaceCore}. Assume that $i(\Phi)$ is dense in $\mathcal H$. For any complex
linear endomorphism $A:\Phi\to\Phi$, define the partial Hilbert-space operator $T_A$ by
\[
  \mathcal D(T_A)=\operatorname{ran} i,
  \qquad T_A(i\phi)=i(A\phi).
\]
Then $\mathcal D(T_A)$ is dense in $\mathcal H$.

\section{Source, assumptions, and statement delta}
This is a source-neutral functional-analytic bridge to Mathlib's \texttt{LinearPMap} API. The dense
range and injectivity of $i$ are explicit structure fields; the theorem introduces no physical
postulate. The operator $A$ is only linear. Its continuity, boundedness, symmetry, closedness, and
closability are neither assumed nor concluded. No governed textbook equation or protected source
expression is claimed.

\section{Prior art and contribution boundary}
The density of the range of a dense embedding is standard. The repository contribution is the exact
connection between \texttt{RiggedHilbertSpaceCore} and the domain representation used by Mathlib's
partial linear maps, not a novelty claim for this elementary fact.

\section{Proof schematic}
\[
\operatorname{Set.range}(i)\ \text{dense}
\quad\land\quad
\operatorname{Set.range}(i)\subseteq\operatorname{LinearMap.range}(i)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\operatorname{LinearMap.range}(i)\ \text{dense}.
\]
By definition, the rightmost set is precisely $\mathcal D(T_A)$.

\section{Human-readable proof}
Every vector in the ordinary image of $i$ has the form $i\phi$. The same witness $\phi$ shows that
this vector belongs to the linear-map range of $i$. Thus the dense ordinary image of $i$ is a subset
of the domain of $T_A$. Any set containing a dense subset is dense, so $\mathcal D(T_A)$ is dense.
The argument is independent of the action of $A$.
