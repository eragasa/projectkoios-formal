\section{Exact statement}
If $x\in B(c_1,r_1)$ and $y\in B(c_2,r_2)$, then
\[
 \max\{0,d(c_1,c_2)-r_1-r_2\}\le d(x,y).
\]
No radius-sign premise is needed for this direction; membership supplies the required
bounds.

\section{Proof}
The triangle inequality gives
$d(c_1,c_2)\le d(c_1,x)+d(x,y)+d(y,c_2)$. Membership bounds the first and last terms by
$r_1$ and $r_2$. Rearrangement gives the center-gap bound, while nonnegativity of distance
gives the zero branch of the maximum.
