\section{Exact mathematical statement}
Fix one model $m$, candidate $c$, alignment $a$, and exact upper-budget record
$b=(b_{\rm int},b_{\rm conv},b_{\rm trunc},b_{\rm total})$. Let the corresponding exact
finite-maximum residual values be $v_{\rm int},v_{\rm conv},v_{\rm trunc},v_{\rm total}$.
Assume explicit total headroom
\[
 b_{\rm int}+b_{\rm conv}+b_{\rm trunc}\le b_{\rm total}.
\]
Then acceptance by the narrow four-channel adapter is equivalent to
\[
 v_{\rm int}\le b_{\rm int}\ \land\
 v_{\rm conv}\le b_{\rm conv}\ \land\
 v_{\rm trunc}\le b_{\rm trunc}.
\]
The total-channel inequality follows from these three inequalities and headroom; it is not deleted
without that premise.

\section{Proof structure}
Expanding four-channel acceptance yields the three component inequalities and the total inequality.
The forward direction discards only the already-proved total conjunct. For the reverse direction,
the exact residual-field decomposition first gives the finite-maximum triangle bound
\[
 v_{\rm total}\le v_{\rm int}+v_{\rm conv}+v_{\rm trunc}.
\]
Coordinatewise component passage bounds this sum by the component budget sum, and the explicit
headroom premise bounds that sum by $b_{\rm total}$. The original four-channel acceptance theorem
then closes the result.
