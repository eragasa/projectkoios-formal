\section{Statement}
For any decision type and two predicates $A,B$ on its decisions, at least one declared
classification holds: the inventory is empty; the first channel is empty; the first is
nonempty and the second is empty; both are individually nonempty but have no common
witness; or a common witness exists.
\section{Argument}
First split on whether the decision type is inhabited. For an inhabited type, split on
existence of an $A$ witness and then a $B$ witness. If both exist, split on existence of a
single decision satisfying $A\land B$. These exhaustive cases construct exactly the
corresponding classification witness.
\section{Boundary and provenance}
The theorem classifies arbitrary predicates logically. It does not prove that objective
values, thresholds, or inventory coverage are correct, and it gives no authority beyond
the declared decision type. Lean checked the exact statement; the dossier and proof were
AI-assisted.
