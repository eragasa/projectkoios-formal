\section{Exact mathematical statement}
Fix one model class.  Assume, for every candidate in its declared finite candidate inventory, that:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the candidate's alignment family is finite; and
\item every exact minimizer of the aligned-operator loss passes all mandatory channels.
\end{enumerate}
Then the model is centrally compatible---one common candidate passes the spectral budget and the alignment-infimum operator budget with a nonempty family---if and only if it is witness-compatible---one common candidate and one concrete alignment pass both budgets and all mandatory channels.

\section{Assumptions, conventions, and statement delta}
Central compatibility already supplies nonemptiness for the common candidate's family.  Finiteness then supplies an exact minimizer; the independent minimizer-channel premise makes that same alignment conformant.  Through the precise tagged adapter, conformance may mean all channel-specific exact error inequalities and all separately typed structural requirements.  The theorem does not infer either class of obligation from finiteness and does not equate infimum and witness semantics for infinite families or without channel conformance.

\section{Proof structure and mathematical argument}
For the forward direction, central compatibility provides one common candidate with spectral and central operator acceptance.  Finite-family attainment provides a loss-minimizing alignment, central passage places its loss within budget, and the explicit channel hypothesis supplies every mandatory channel for that same minimizer.  The resulting candidate and alignment establish witness compatibility.  The reverse direction is the existing generic witness-to-central implication and needs no finiteness premise.
