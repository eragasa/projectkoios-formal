\section{Exact mathematical statement}

Let $X$ be a metric space, let $A,B\subseteq X$, and define the extended admissible-set
distance
\[
 \delta_{\mathrm e}(A,B)
 =\inf_{a\in A,\,b\in B} d_{\mathrm e}(a,b),
\]
with values in $[0,\infty]$. For every $a\in A$ and $b\in B$,
\[
 \delta_{\mathrm e}(A,B)\le d_{\mathrm e}(a,b).
\]

In the scientific application, $A$ and $B$ are the spectral and aligned-operator
admissible sets inside one declared candidate model-parameter domain. A feasible pair
supplies an upper bound only; it is not a joint match unless the same candidate belongs
to both sets.

\section{Human-readable proof}

The admissible-set distance is the infimum of all extended distances between a member of
$A$ and a member of $B$. The hypotheses place $(a,b)$ among those feasible pairs. An
infimum is no greater than any element of the family over which it is taken. Therefore
$\delta_{\mathrm e}(A,B)\le d_{\mathrm e}(a,b)$.
