\section{Exact mathematical statement}
For nonempty compact subsets $A,B$ of a metric space,
\[
  \delta_{\rm e}(A,B)=0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad A\cap B\ne\varnothing.
\]
The conclusion is literal intersection, not compatibility at a positive map tolerance.

\section{Proof argument}
A common point gives zero distance by evaluating the infimum at that point twice. For
the converse, suppose the distance is zero but the intersection is empty. Empty
intersection makes the sets disjoint. The existing compact-separation theorem then gives
strictly positive distance, contradicting zero. Compactness and nonemptiness are exactly
what exclude distinct nonintersecting sets with zero infimum distance and the empty-set
value $\top$.
