\section{Exact mathematical statement}

Let $X$ be a metric space and let $A,B\subseteq X$ be nonempty compact disjoint sets.
Then there are $a\in A$ and $b\in B$ such that
\[
 \delta_{\mathrm e}(A,B)=d_{\mathrm e}(a,b)
 \qquad\text{and}\qquad
 0<\delta_{\mathrm e}(A,B).
\]
Thus the extended admissible-set distance is attained and strictly positive.

\section{Human-readable proof}

The product $A\times B$ is nonempty and compact. Extended distance is continuous on the
product, so the extreme-value theorem supplies a minimizing pair $(a,b)$. The definition
of $\delta_{\mathrm e}$ gives one inequality between the infimum and
$d_{\mathrm e}(a,b)$; minimality gives the reverse inequality. Hence the values are
equal. If $a=b$, that point belongs to both $A$ and $B$, contradicting disjointness.
Therefore $a\ne b$, and the metric-space self-separation law gives
$0<d_{\mathrm e}(a,b)=\delta_{\mathrm e}(A,B)$.
