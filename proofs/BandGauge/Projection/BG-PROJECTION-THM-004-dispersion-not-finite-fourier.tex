\section{Exact mathematical statement}
Let $\Delta,t\in\mathbb R$ with $\Delta t\ne0$ and
\[
 d(k)=\sqrt{\Delta^2+4t^2\cos^2(k/2)}.
\]
There is no $f\in\mathbb C[z,z^{-1}]$ such that
$f(e^{ik})=d(k)$ for every $k\in\mathbb R$.

\section{Proof schematic and argument}
Assume $f(e^{ik})=d(k)$. Squaring gives
\[
 f(e^{ik})^2=\Delta^2+4t^2\cos^2(k/2)
 =\Delta^2+2t^2+t^2(e^{ik}+e^{-ik}).
\]
By unit-circle injectivity, $f^2$ equals the source Laurent radicand in
$\mathbb C[z,z^{-1}]$. This contradicts \path{BG-PROJECTION-THM-002}, which excludes a
Laurent square root under $\Delta t\ne0$.
