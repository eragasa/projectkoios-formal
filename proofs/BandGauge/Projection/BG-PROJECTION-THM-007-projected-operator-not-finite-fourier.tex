\section{Exact mathematical statement}
For real $\Delta,t$ with $\Delta t\ne0$, the matrix circle function
\[
 K(k)=\frac12\bigl(H(k)-d(k)I\bigr)
\]
has no representation by a $2\times2$ matrix over
$\mathbb C[z,z^{-1}]$ evaluated at $z=e^{ik}$.

\section{Proof schematic and argument}
Assume a Laurent-polynomial matrix $M(z)$ represents $K(k)$. Its $(1,1)$ entry satisfies
\[
 M_{11}(e^{ik})=\frac12(\Delta-d(k)).
\]
Therefore the Laurent polynomial $\Delta-2M_{11}(z)$ represents $d(k)$ on the unit circle,
contradicting \path{BG-PROJECTION-THM-004}. Hence no such matrix exists.
