\section{Exact mathematical statement}
Let $K$ be a finite mesh and let $A_k,B_k\in\mathbb C^{m\times n}$ for every $k\in K$.
For a unitary field $U=(U_k)_{k\in K}$ define the unweighted summed objective
\[
  J_K(U)=\sum_{k\in K}\lVert A_kU_k-B_k\rVert_F^2.
\]
Suppose that at every mesh point exact data $Q_k,H_k$ satisfy
\[
  Q_k^*Q_k=I,\qquad H_k\succeq0,\qquad A_k^*B_k=Q_kH_k.
\]
Then the pointwise polar-witness field $Q=(Q_k)_{k\in K}$ is a global minimizer:
\[
  J_K(Q)\le J_K(U)
\]
for every unitary field $U$. No cross-point regularity or common-unitary restriction is imposed.

\section{Assumptions and conventions}
The mesh and matrix dimensions are finite. Each $Q_k$ is exactly unitary and each $H_k$ is exactly
positive semidefinite. The objective is an equal-weight finite sum of squared Frobenius norms. The
theorem does not infer mesh weights, continuity, sewing, or a relation among different $Q_k$.

\section{Proof structure}
For every $k$, the supplied polar witness and \texttt{BG-ALIGNMENT-THM-044} give
\[
  \lVert A_kQ_k-B_k\rVert_F^2
  \le \lVert A_kU_k-B_k\rVert_F^2.
\]
Finite sums preserve pointwise inequalities. Summing this inequality over all $k\in K$ gives the
claimed global inequality. No attainment argument or runtime optimizer is needed because the
minimizing field is part of the exact supplied witness.
