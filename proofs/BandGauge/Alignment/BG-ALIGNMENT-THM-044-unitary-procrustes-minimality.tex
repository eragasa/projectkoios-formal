\section{Exact mathematical statement}
Let $A,B\in\mathbb C^{m\times n}$. For a unitary $R\in\mathbb C^{n\times n}$ define
\[
  J(R)=\lVert AR-B\rVert_F^2.
\]
Suppose exact data $Q,H$ satisfy
\[
  Q^*Q=I,\qquad H\succeq0,\qquad A^*B=QH.
\]
Then for every unitary $R$,
\[
  J(Q)\le J(R).
\]
The action is on the right and the cross matrix is $A^*B$.

\section{Proof structure}
First expand
\[
  J(R)=\lVert A\rVert_F^2+\lVert B\rVert_F^2
       -2\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{tr}(R^*A^*B).
\]
The first two terms are independent of $R$. Substituting $A^*B=QH$ reduces the score for $R$ to
$\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{tr}(R^*QH)$. The relative matrix $R^*Q$ is unitary. Diagonalize
$H$ with the finite Hermitian spectral theorem. Its eigenvalues are nonnegative, and each diagonal
entry of a unitary has real part at most one, so
\[
  \operatorname{Re}\operatorname{tr}(R^*QH)\le\operatorname{tr}(H).
\]
At $R=Q$, the score is exactly $\operatorname{tr}(H)$. Reversing the sign of the score term gives
$J(Q)\le J(R)$.
