\section{Exact mathematical statement}
Let $M$ be a finite inventory in an ambient mode type $\mathcal X$, let
$\rho:\mathcal X\to L$ take values in a linearly ordered radius type, and define
\[
  \operatorname{shell}(S)=\{R\in M:\rho(R)\le S\},\qquad
  \operatorname{attained}(M)=\{\rho(R):R\in M\}.
\]
For cutoffs $S\le T$,
\[
 \operatorname{shell}(S)\ne\operatorname{shell}(T)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \exists r\in\operatorname{attained}(M),\ S<r\le T.
\]
Thus a support change is neither inferred from a continuous radius model nor hidden between
attained values: it has an exact witness in the declared finite inventory.

\section{Assumptions and conventions}
The inventory is a Lean \texttt{Finset}; finiteness is therefore part of the type. Radius values
are exact and linearly ordered. Both shells use the same inventory and radius function. No metric,
negation, symmetry, geometry, or nonemptiness assumption is needed for this theorem.

\section{Proof structure}
First prove the element-witness form
\[
 \operatorname{shell}(S)\ne\operatorname{shell}(T)
 \Longleftrightarrow
 \exists R\in M,\ S<\rho(R)\le T.
\]
If no such $R$ exists, monotonicity gives
$\operatorname{shell}(S)\subseteq\operatorname{shell}(T)$, while every member of the second shell
must also have radius at most $S$; hence the shells are equal. Conversely, a mode in the displayed
radius gap belongs to the second shell and not the first. Mapping the witness $R$ to $r=\rho(R)$
gives an attained radius. Membership in the finite image supplies the reverse witness.
