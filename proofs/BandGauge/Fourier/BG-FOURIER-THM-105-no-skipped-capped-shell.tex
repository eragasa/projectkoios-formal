\section{Exact mathematical statement}
Let $M$ be a finite mode inventory, $\rho$ an exact linearly ordered radius, and
\[
 \operatorname{shell}(S)=\{R\in M:\rho(R)\le S\},\qquad
 \operatorname{attained}_{\le C}(M)=\{r:\exists R\in M,\ r=\rho(R)\ \text{and}\ r\le C\}.
\]
If $S\le T\le C$ and the shells at $S$ and $T$ differ, then there exists
$r\in\operatorname{attained}_{\le C}(M)$ with
\[
  S<r\le T.
\]
Consequently, every support change observed through the cap is represented in the exact capped
attained-radius inventory.

\section{Assumptions and conventions}
The theorem assumes one finite inventory, one exact radius function, ordered cutoffs
$S\le T\le C$, and an actual inequality of the two exact supports. It does not assume that every
value in the ambient ordered type is attainable. Empty inventories are allowed, but then the shell
inequality premise is impossible and yields no external coverage claim.

\section{Proof structure}
Apply \texttt{BG-FOURIER-THM-100} to the distinct shells. This gives an attained witness radius
$r$ with $S<r\le T$. Since $T\le C$, transitivity gives $r\le C$. The same radius witness therefore
belongs to the finite filtered image $\operatorname{attained}_{\le C}(M)$, while preserving both
gap inequalities. The proof is an exact finite consequence, not an enumeration algorithm.
