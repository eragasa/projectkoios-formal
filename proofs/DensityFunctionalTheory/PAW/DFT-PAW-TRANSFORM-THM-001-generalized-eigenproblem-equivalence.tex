\section{Exact mathematical statement}
Let $I$ be a finite index type, let $T,H\in\mathbb C^{I\times I}$, let
$\widetilde\psi\in\mathbb C^I$, and let $\varepsilon\in\mathbb C$.  Define
\[
 \psi=T\widetilde\psi,\qquad
 \widetilde H=T^\dagger H T,\qquad
 S=T^\dagger T.
\]
If $T$ is invertible, then
\[
 \widetilde H\widetilde\psi=\varepsilon S\widetilde\psi
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 H\psi=\varepsilon\psi.
\]
No Hermiticity or normalization premise is needed for this equivalence.

\section{Proof schematic}
For the forward direction,
\[
 H(T\widetilde\psi)=\varepsilon(T\widetilde\psi)
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 T^\dagger H T\widetilde\psi=\varepsilon T^\dagger T\widetilde\psi.
\]
For the converse, the generalized equation says
\[
 T^\dagger\bigl(H(T\widetilde\psi)\bigr)
 =T^\dagger\bigl(\varepsilon(T\widetilde\psi)\bigr).
\]
Invertibility of $T$ implies injectivity of the action of $T^\dagger$, so it can be cancelled.

\section{Human-readable mathematical argument}
Substitute the definitions of the transformed operator and overlap.  Any reconstructed-state
eigenpair remains an eigenpair after applying $T^\dagger$ to both sides, giving the generalized
equation without an invertibility assumption.  Conversely, if $T$ is invertible, then so is
$T^\dagger$; hence its matrix action is injective.  Cancelling that action from the generalized
equation recovers the standard eigenproblem for the reconstructed state.
