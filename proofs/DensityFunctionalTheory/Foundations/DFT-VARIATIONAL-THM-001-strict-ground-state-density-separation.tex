\section{Exact mathematical statement}
Let \(S,D,V\) be arbitrary types and let a variational model supply a density map
\(n:S\to D\), an internal energy \(F:S\to\mathbb R\), and an external energy
\(E_{\rm ext}:V\to D\to\mathbb R\).  If \(s_1\ne s_2\), \(s_1\) is a strict global
minimizer of
\[
  F(s)+E_{\rm ext}(v_1,n(s)),
\]
and \(s_2\) is a strict global minimizer of the analogous energy for \(v_2\), then
\[
  n(s_1)\ne n(s_2).
\]
The state carrier must already identify any irrelevant equivalences, such as global phase, that a
concrete model does not wish strict minimization to distinguish.

\section{Proof schematic}
\[
\begin{array}{c}
 s_1\ne s_2,\quad n(s_1)=n(s_2)\\[2pt]
 \Downarrow\\
 E_{v_1}(s_1)<E_{v_1}(s_2),\quad E_{v_2}(s_2)<E_{v_2}(s_1)\\[2pt]
 \Downarrow\ \text{equal density cancels both external terms}\\
 F(s_1)<F(s_2),\quad F(s_2)<F(s_1)\\[2pt]
 \Downarrow\\
 \bot
\end{array}
\]

\section{Human-readable mathematical argument}
Assume for contradiction that the two densities agree.  Strict minimality of \(s_1\), tested at
\(s_2\), gives the first strict total-energy inequality.  Strict minimality of \(s_2\), tested at
\(s_1\), gives the reverse inequality for the second potential.  Because the densities agree, the
external-energy term in each inequality is evaluated at the same density on both sides and
cancels.  The remaining inequalities assert both \(F(s_1)<F(s_2)\) and
\(F(s_2)<F(s_1)\), a contradiction.
