\section{Exact mathematical statement}
Let \(S,D,X\) be arbitrary types.  Potentials are functions \(X\to\mathbb R\), and let
\(M\) be a variational density model.  Assume the model has the property
\[
 \bigl(s\text{ is a ground state for }v_1\bigr)\land
 \bigl(s\text{ is a ground state for }v_2\bigr)
 \Longrightarrow
 \exists c\in\mathbb R\;\forall x\in X,\;v_1(x)=v_2(x)+c.
\]
If \(s_1\) and \(s_2\) are strict ground states for \(v_1\) and \(v_2\), respectively, and
\(n(s_1)=n(s_2)\), then \(v_1\) and \(v_2\) differ by one additive constant.

\section{Proof schematic}
\[
\begin{array}{c}
 n(s_1)=n(s_2)\\
 \Downarrow\ \text{case split on }s_1=s_2\\
 \begin{array}{cc}
 s_1=s_2 & s_1\ne s_2\\
 \Downarrow & \Downarrow\\
 \text{shared-state hypothesis} & \text{DFT-VARIATIONAL-THM-001 gives }n(s_1)\ne n(s_2)\\
 \Downarrow & \Downarrow\\
 v_1\sim v_2\pmod{\text{constant}} & \bot
 \end{array}
\end{array}
\]

\section{Human-readable mathematical argument}
Split on whether the two strict ground states are the same state.  If they are equal, each strict
ground state is also a non-strict ground state, so the explicit shared-ground-state hypothesis gives
the additive-constant relation.  If they are distinct, the strict variational theorem says their
densities must differ, contradicting the assumed density equality.  Thus only the first branch is
possible, and the potential equivalence follows.
