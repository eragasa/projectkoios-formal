\begin{abstract}
This working paper organizes an audit-first formalization program for the mathematical,
computational, and evidentiary claims currently inventoried across Ragasa2026A,
Ragasa2026B, and Ragasa2026C. The program separates exact theorem statements from
numerical evidence, calculator authority, scientific interpretation, and publication
status. Its first formal results define an extended distance between two admissible
subsets of one metric model-parameter domain and prove that any feasible pair gives an
upper bound and that one common candidate forces zero distance. These are classical
metric-space facts, not new theory. The contribution is methodological and applied: standard set-distance facts,
feasible-parameter-set language, and validated global certificates form an explicit
decision procedure for joint spectral
and restricted aligned-operator adequacy. A reduced scalar rank-two layer additionally
checks the irreducible operator-loss floor and the below-floor emptiness condition without
obscuring assumptions or evidence boundaries. Unformalized Ragasa claims are reported as
planned, external, or unresolved rather than silently promoted to formal results.
\end{abstract}

\section{Motivation and contribution boundary}

The mathematical ingredients are established.

\begin{itemize}
 \item Elementary facts about the distance between two subsets of a metric space follow
 from the definition of an infimum.
 \item Positive separation of a compact set from a disjoint closed set is standard;
 compact attainment is obtained by minimizing the continuous distance function on a
 compact product.
 \item A failed local or finite search is not a global infeasibility certificate. This
 distinction is standard in complete search, constraint satisfaction, and validated
 global optimization.
 \item Feasible parameter sets, consistency testing, model invalidation, and
 infeasibility are established bounded-error and set-membership-identification ideas.
 \item Validated lower bounds for a set-distance optimization problem use established
 interval and branch-and-bound machinery.
\end{itemize}

This work claims no new theorem or theory and no priority for the witness/infeasibility
distinction. It integrates classical facts and established computational methods into a
threshold-defined rule for joint spectral and aligned-operator adequacy, with replayable
witnesses or lower-bound certificates. A terminology choice or bounded phrase search
establishes no priority.

\section{Scientific decision represented by the first theorem family}

Let $X$ be one declared candidate model-parameter domain. Frozen loss definitions and
thresholds determine
\[
 \mathcal A_E=\{\theta\in X:L_E(\theta)\le\tau_E\},\qquad
 \mathcal A_H=\{\theta\in X:L_{H,\mathrm{orb}}(\theta)\le\tau_H\}.
\]
The first set contains candidates meeting the spectral budget. The second contains
candidates meeting the restricted aligned-operator budget. The alignment loss ranges
only over the declared admissible alignment family.

A common witness is one and the same candidate
$\theta\in\mathcal A_E\cap\mathcal A_H$. Two different candidates, one in each set, do
not establish joint adequacy. A certified positive lower bound on the distance between
the sets proves that no candidate in the declared family meets both budgets. It does not
exclude a larger candidate family, prove material invalidity, establish hopping
locality, or show physical inequivalence of complete gauge-related Hamiltonians.

The decision vocabulary is:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item \textbf{joint match}: an explicit common witness satisfies both budgets;
 \item \textbf{certified separation}: a verified global lower bound exceeds a declared
 separation resolution; and
 \item \textbf{unresolved}: neither qualifying evidence object exists.
\end{enumerate}
A failed optimizer, finite scan, or plot belongs to the third category.

\section{Current formal results}

For a metric space $X$ and subsets $A,B\subseteq X$, the repository defines
\[
 \delta_{\mathrm e}(A,B)=
 \inf_{a\in A,\,b\in B} d_{\mathrm e}(a,b)\in[0,\infty].
\]
The extended codomain preserves the source rule that empty input must not produce a
finite reported set distance. BG-DEF-002 and BG-THM-006 provide the real-valued nonempty-set bridge.

\paragraph{BG-THM-001: feasible-pair upper bound.}
If $a\in A$ and $b\in B$, then
\[
 \delta_{\mathrm e}(A,B)\le d_{\mathrm e}(a,b).
\]
The chosen pair is one member of the family over which the infimum is taken.

\paragraph{BG-THM-002: common witness.}
If $\theta\in A\cap B$, then
\[
 \delta_{\mathrm e}(A,B)=0.
\]
Apply BG-THM-001 to $(\theta,\theta)$ and use nonnegativity and
$d_{\mathrm e}(\theta,\theta)=0$.

\paragraph{BG-THM-003: certified separation.}
If $0<\ell\le\delta_{\mathrm e}(A,B)$, then $A$ and $B$ are disjoint. A common member
would force the distance to zero by BG-THM-002, contradicting the positive lower bound.

\paragraph{BG-THM-004: compact attainment and positivity.}
Nonempty compact disjoint sets admit $a\in A$ and $b\in B$ such that
$\delta_{\mathrm e}(A,B)=d_{\mathrm e}(a,b)>0$. The proof minimizes continuous extended
distance on the compact product $A\times B$.

\paragraph{BG-THM-005: nonempty finiteness.}
Nonempty $A$ and $B$ satisfy $\delta_{\mathrm e}(A,B)\ne\infty$.

\paragraph{BG-DEF-002 and BG-THM-006: real/extended bridge.}
BG-DEF-002 exposes a real-valued distance only with nonemptiness proofs. BG-THM-006 proves
that conversion back to the extended nonnegative reals recovers BG-DEF-001.

\paragraph{BG-DEF-003/004 and BG-THM-007/008: threshold-defined adequacy.}
BG-DEF-003 and BG-DEF-004 define the spectral and restricted aligned-operator loss
sublevel sets on one candidate domain. BG-THM-007 turns joint loss bounds into zero set
distance. BG-THM-008 turns certified positive separation into nonexistence of a jointly
admissible candidate.

\paragraph{BG-DEF-005--017 and BG-THM-009--034: reduced rank-two loss and ellipse geometry.}
BG-DEF-005 uses the Euclidean shift/splitting-scale plane. BG-DEF-006 and BG-DEF-007
encode the published reduced scalar squared-loss formulas. BG-THM-009 proves the
operator-loss floor for nonnegative curvature, and BG-THM-010 proves that a squared
budget strictly below that floor makes the operator admissible set empty. BG-DEF-008 and
BG-THM-011 isolate the scalar normalization step from the still-external matrix
correlation bound. BG-THM-012--014 prove floor and squared-loss nonnegativity.
BG-DEF-009/010 expose nonnegative real losses, while BG-THM-015--018 prove
square-root recovery and equivalence between real-loss and squared-loss thresholds.
BG-DEF-011 and BG-THM-019--021 expose the operator radius and the explicit spectral and
operator ellipse inequalities. BG-DEF-012/013 and BG-THM-022--026 prove the necessary,
strict-separation, constructive-sufficiency, exact full-ellipse, and reduced real-loss
intersection criteria. BG-DEF-014 and BG-THM-027--030 prove the pairwise lower bound,
constructive attainment, exact full-ellipse distance, and reduced real-loss distance.
BG-DEF-015--017 define the closed candidate rectangle and its restricted ellipses.

\paragraph{BG-BLOCH-DEF-001--011 and BG-BLOCH-THM-001--011: pointwise sewing and finite samples.}
As supporting library infrastructure, rather than a Paper A--C contribution, the Bloch
layer defines a unitary reciprocal-group cocycle, sewn sections and operator fields,
sewing-compatible alignments, and aligned operator pullback. It also defines an abstract
sewn Hermitian rank-two Hamiltonian with a finite normalized weighted sample, its
trace-centered matrix field, and source-oriented unitary conjugation. The proved results
establish covariance preservation, tracelessness, unitary power invariance, common
power, and the correlation bound. These standard linear-algebraic and solid-state
interfaces carry no novelty claim. This is an interface rather than a Bloch--Floquet
construction from a periodic potential. Regularity, quotient-torus,
